Design Overview:Why a stock's PVGO share moves

We track a stock's growth share daily: the fraction of the price above its no-growth value, trailing earnings capitalised at the cost of equity with no growth. Every daily change is bridged into market, company-specific, earnings and rate parts. Each month is cut into periods labelled by how much of the move the market explains, and every period and every earnings release is explained by AI that works only from a controlled set of sources, filtered through the company's value drivers, and must cite the evidence behind every cause. Ask a chatbot why a stock moved and you get a plausible answer from opaque sources; here every answer can be checked line by line.

no-growth value  = adjusted TTM EPS ÷ cost of equity cost of equity   = 10y Treasury + β × ERP growth share     = 1 − no-growth value ÷ price  (= PVGO ÷ price)

Two models, each matched to its job

Step 1 · Extraction

Qwen3-235B open-weight, via DeepInfra

Task
High volume, narrow job: extract statements from each article and code them to the codebook, returned as schema-enforced JSON.
Why
The task is tightly constrained, so a large open-weight model does it at a fraction of frontier prices. It answers with sentence numbers, so every quote is verbatim.
Step 4 · Explanation

Claude Sonnet Anthropic, batch API

Task
A few dozen calls a year that need judgment: one per period (44 for Costco's year), naming the change in expectations behind its move, and one per earnings release, which only extracts the one-time items its press releases name. Outputs are structured and validated in code.
Why
This is where model quality matters most. Running the calls as one batch job lowers the price.

A cheap model where volume is high and the task is constrained, a stronger one where volume is low and judgment matters. Each paid step shows its cost estimate and asks before spending. The extraction model is a setting, so Sonnet can run step 1 too.

Why the output can be trusted

  • A structured codebook, not free-form taggingCodes follow Damodaran's value drivers, with definitions and routing rules, enforced by a JSON schema. Stock-pick pages and bare price-move or rating statements are excluded.
  • Point in timeEPS uses only filings available at each date, stepped at the 8-K acceptance time. Beta uses only prices known at each month end. Each period's explanation reads only its own news, from the day before to the day after.
  • Every claim has a sourceQuotes are verbatim by construction. Each cause carries a statement ID that traces to its article and outlet, and each release cites its press releases, linked to SEC EDGAR.
  • A placebo check, built inThe news is company-specific, so a cause found where the market or ordinary noise explains the move is suspect. In Costco's year the model found one in 1 of 34 such periods, against 4 of the 8 that stand out. Given fake moves on 12 quiet days, it found a medium-confidence cause for 1.

What better data would add

The prototype runs on free sources: three outlets on Media Cloud whose full text can be fetched without a paywall, selected by a search term in the headline. Nothing downstream depends on the source. A licensed news feed would plug into step 1 unchanged.

We expect it to widen coverage and cut screening noise. In the Costco run, 36 of 44 periods have no cause found and one more had no news, and none of the six stand-out rises has a cause in the news. Part of that is drift that news may never explain, which is why estimate revisions and style factors are also on the roadmap.
